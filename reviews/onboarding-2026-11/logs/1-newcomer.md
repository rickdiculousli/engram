# Log: Persona 1, curious newcomer (event planner, uses the Claude app, never opened a terminal)

## 1. Path

1. **Opened the link from my friend (~3 s).** The top of the page is GitHub's own stuff: menus, "Sign in", "Products", "Copilot". I ignored all of it and scrolled to the big picture. (readme-00)
2. **The banner (~10 s).** An orange brain made of letters with stuff raining through it, and the word "engram" in chunky pixel letters. Read the two lines under it: "Bring your knowledge base, or start fresh. Recall every thread and aside, in fewer tokens." Then four orange words: "keep  find  rewire  evolve". (readme-01, top)
3. **The little badges (~2 s).** "python 3.12+", "index SQLite FTS5", "record plain git", "license MIT". Skipped them; they look like part numbers.
4. **The first paragraph (~15 s).** "engram files what you tell Claude Code into plain git, and hands Claude the whole story when you ask." Okay, "hands Claude the whole story" is exactly what I want. But "Claude Code"? Is that the coding version? And "plain git"? Then "finds what grep finds for just over half the tokens". Lost me.
5. **The "For" line (~10 s).** "For one person using Claude Code on their own computer: macOS, Linux, or Windows through WSL2* ... Not the Claude app or claude.ai." I read "Not the Claude app or claude.ai" twice. That's the only Claude I use. Hovered the blue "Claude Code" link, didn't click; it's obviously for coders.
6. **Skimmed down out of politeness to my friend (~20 s).** Bullets ("Only what you said", "Corrections kept") sounded nice but abstract. The diagram ("a session", "hourly filer", "/[latest].md", "git commit + push", "index ... linked as a graph") looked like a flowchart for engineers. Scrolled into "Find" and saw `$ uv run engram timeline "what happened with the north bed this season?" --domain notes` and a table of ids like "p8qdad". That's a programmer screen. (readme-01 bottom, readme-02)
7. **Closed the tab (~1 min 05 s total).**

## 2. First impression (first 10 seconds)

Cool-looking brain art, a little "hacker". With "engram" and "keep / find", I guessed it's some memory add-on for Claude, which is what my friend said. "Start fresh" made me think: good, I don't need to have anything set up already. For a second I was hopeful.

## 3. Confusions

- **"Bring your knowledge base"** (banner). I don't have a "knowledge base". Is that a work thing? "Or start fresh" saved it; I guessed that meant I'm fine with nothing.
- **"Recall every thread and aside, in fewer tokens"** (banner). "Thread" I get (a conversation). "Aside"? Like a side comment I made? "Tokens": no idea. Arcade tokens? Do I pay with tokens? It sounds like a cost I'd have to understand.
- **"keep / find / rewire / evolve"** (banner and nav). Keep and find, sure. "Rewire" and "evolve" sound like it changes things on its own, which is a bit spooky for my health stuff.
- **Badges "python 3.12+", "SQLite FTS5", "plain git", "MIT"** (under banner). Gibberish. Python I've heard is coding.
- **"Claude Code"** (first paragraph). I assumed it's Claude, but for code. Not sure if it's a different product from the app I pay for.
- **"files ... into plain git"** (first paragraph). "Git" is the GitHub thing? So my trip plans would go onto GitHub where code lives? Is that public?
- **"finds what grep finds for just over half the tokens"** (first paragraph). "Grep" sounds like a made-up word. Couldn't guess. "(measured)" link: not clicking, it'll be charts.
- **"macOS, Linux, or Windows through WSL2*"** ("For" line). Mac I know. Linux is for nerds. "WSL2" with an asterisk: no clue. The asterisk made me think there's a catch.
- **"one repo per area of your life"** (bullet). "Repo" again. I guessed it's a folder? "Area of your life" was the first phrase that sounded like *me* (health, money, travel).
- **"a day you never gave → the month you did"** (bullet). Couldn't parse it. Read it twice. Something about dates?
- **"reshape a repo around the questions you bring it, on a copy first"** (Rewire). No idea what I'd be reshaping.
- **"a weekly pass under a dollar cap you set"** (Evolve). Wait, it costs money? How much? Who charges me?
- **The diagram** ("hourly filer", "/[latest].md", "git commit + push", "indexed", "linked as a graph"). The animation showed "me: [knowledge]" with green ticks, which I sort of got (it saves what I said). The rest was a wiring diagram.
- **"$ uv run engram timeline ... --domain notes"** (Find). That's the terminal. That's where I stopped trying.

## 4. The value moment

Almost, at: **"hands Claude the whole story when you ask."** That's exactly my story: Claude forgets my stuff by the next chat, and I want it to just know. Then, two lines later: **"Not the Claude app or claude.ai."** So the value was for someone else. Net: **never**, for me. It matches what I want, but it plainly says it doesn't work where I use Claude.

## 5. Claims

- **Believed:** "Not the Claude app or claude.ai." The clearest sentence on the page, and I took it at face value. Also believed it's made for people who code, because everything below looked like code.
- **Believed, sort of:** "Only what you said" / "in your own words". Sounded reassuring (it won't make stuff up about me), but I didn't know how it would actually work.
- **Didn't understand:** "fewer tokens", "what grep finds for just over half the tokens", "measured". These are clearly the big brag, and they meant nothing to me.
- **Doubted / worried:** "Evolve ... a dollar cap you set". It made me think this costs money on top of my Claude subscription. Also "plain git" plus "push" made me worry my health stuff would end up on GitHub for everyone.
- **Didn't understand:** "Rewire", "Corrections kept ... the newest in full", "one call → the match and what it links to".

## 6. Exit

Closed the tab at about 1 minute, right after skimming into the "Find" section and seeing the terminal command. The real decision came earlier, at "Not the Claude app or claude.ai." Everything after that was me checking whether I'd misread. No star, no bookmark, no install. (I didn't try installing, and I wouldn't: I've never opened a terminal and this persona never would.) I'd text my friend "I don't think that's for me."

## 7. In your own words

"Your link is cool-looking but it's for programmers. It literally says it's not for the Claude app, only some 'Claude Code' thing you run on your computer with commands. Also it talks about tokens and git and a dollar cap, so I couldn't even tell what it'd cost. Is there something like that that just works in the normal app?"

## 8. What would have kept / convinced you

- Tell me in the first line, in plain words, who this is for: "This is for people who use Claude Code (the coding tool on your computer). If you use the Claude app on your phone, this isn't for you; try ___ instead." Then I'd leave in 10 seconds, not 60, and not feel dumb.
- If there's any way someone like me could use it (someone sets it up for me, or a version in the app), say that right at the top with one button, not a terminal command.
- Show me a normal example from someone's life before any code: "Monday you mention your knee hurts. Three weeks later you ask 'when did my knee start hurting?' and Claude knows." The tomato garden example would have worked if it was a chat, not a table with ids.
- Don't make "tokens", "grep", "git" and "repo" the selling points. Tell me what I get: "Claude remembers, and it doesn't make things up about you."
- Tell me straight up if it costs money and if my stuff stays private (not on GitHub for everyone).
